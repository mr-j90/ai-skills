01The plan
Meridian is retiring the legacy scanner estate. Shipment telemetry moves with it, so every application reading scan events from the on-prem collector today has to be repointed. 5 March is when UAT begins, which means this has to be live and carrying data by then, not merely built.

Rather than build and maintain an integration inside each application, we land the telemetry record in the data platform once, and every application reads it from there over a standard endpoint. This is what we discussed on 18 December. Two workstreams, running in parallel, on one engagement. Production cutover follows your UAT.

Where the boundary sits

We build
  • The platform workspaces, the ingestion pipeline, and the serving endpoint
  • A managed secret store for the collector credentials, so nothing is stored in code
  • Data quality checks that stop bad data reaching your applications
  • Failure, retry and freshness notifications, raised by email into your existing process
  • The runbook, and knowledge transfer to your platform lead alongside the build
We do not
  • Touch your production data. We build and test against the sandbox and sample data. You execute the promotion to production; we are not members of that workspace.
  • Modify the consuming applications. Your team owns the repoint.
  • Build the network estate. Firewall, private link and DNS remain with your infrastructure team.
  • Provide reporting or analytics on this data. This workspace is operational only.

What each component is

1APlatform and connectivity
Everything your developers need in order to connect. This does not wait on the collector; it starts on day one.
  • Platform workspaces, development and production
  • The service identities your applications authenticate with
  • A managed secret store holding the collector credentials, so nothing sits in code
  • The private link into your network, worked jointly with your network team
  • The endpoint your applications read from
  • Access control over who and what can see the data
  • The development-to-production release path
1BSource read and platform write
The ingestion line. The one component that depends on collector API access being provisioned.
  • A scheduled process that calls the collector API and pulls the telemetry record
  • Authentication, paging and retry handling
  • The record written into a staging area, separate from what your applications read
  • The refresh schedule, starting at 15 minutes and tunable by configuration
1CData quality and validation
The layer between staging and what your applications read. Deliberate, given that this record feeds billing-adjacent systems.
  • A set of checks run against every load, before anything is published
  • If the checks do not pass, the serving table is not updated. Your applications keep serving the last known-good record rather than a bad one
  • Records that fail are held back and reported rather than silently dropped
  • An email notification when a load is stopped, a run fails, or the data goes stale. Where that email goes on your side, and what it opens, stays with your existing process
1DConsumer read and enablement
What takes your application team off the critical path.
  • The endpoint contract: what your applications connect to, how they authenticate, and what they get back
  • A connection guide with working examples
  • A full-size sample data set, available before real telemetry exists
  • Per-application access, so each system sees only the population it should
  • Support for your developers through the build

What done looks like

  • Your developers can connect to the endpoint from inside your network, authenticate, and read the telemetry record.
  • Each application sees only the population it is entitled to, once you have confirmed how those populations divide.
  • The pipeline runs on a 15-minute cycle, tunable by configuration.
  • A deliberately corrupted or truncated response from the collector does not reach your applications, and does raise a notification.
  • A pipeline that fails sends a notification. A pipeline that silently never runs sends one too.
  • Record counts and a field-level sample reconcile against the collector.
  • Your platform lead has the runbook and has worked alongside the build.

04Workstream 2 · Platform governance
Workstream 2 starts 8 February and runs to 30 April, with modules built and deployed as they complete rather than everything arriving at the end.

Two things in it that touch Workstream 1, so they are worth stating now

These two cannot wait for the Workstream 2 detail, because they affect what your team sees and when.

Starts in Workstream 1, week one
Activity and audit log capture
The platform activity API retains roughly 30 days of history. Every day the capture is not running is a day of history that does not exist and cannot be recovered later. So this one piece stands up in the first week of Workstream 1, even though the rest of the governance work starts in February. It costs almost nothing to do early and it cannot be undone later.
Lands end of March
Platform observability and access alerting
To be precise about which alerting is which. Pipeline health alerting is Workstream 1 and ships with the build — a failed run, a stalled run, a failed data quality check and a stale serving table all raise an alert from day one. The layer above that — platform observability, and time-boxed elevation to administrator with alerting on it — is a Workstream 2 delivery, scheduled for end of March, while you are in UAT.

Said plainly

The governance and security layer over this data is only partially delivered inside Workstream 1 — enough to be defensible on day one — and is fully delivered in Workstream 2. We would rather say that now than have it discovered in April.

Running the two together is what gives us the two months after 5 March to finish the governance build properly, while you are in UAT and we are still engaged to support you on Workstream 1.
